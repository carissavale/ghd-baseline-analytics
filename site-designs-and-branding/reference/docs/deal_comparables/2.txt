CLOSEST ACIS COMPARABLE
NFL "Digital Athlete" + AWS $60M+ program
Ongoing since 2019 | League-wide AI injury prediction platform | All 32 NFL teams
WHAT IT IS
The Digital Athlete is an AI-powered injury prediction platform built jointly by the NFL and Amazon Web Services (AWS). It creates a virtual simulation of every NFL
player by combining multiple data sources: RFID tracking data from chips in players' shoulder pads (provided by Zebra Technologies), footage from 38 5K optical
tracking cameras per field capturing 60 frames per second, weather and environmental data, equipment specifications, play type classifications, and aggregated
anonymized injury data across the league. AWS applies machine learning and computer vision to this data to run millions of game simulations, identifying which
players are at highest risk of injury based on their workload, movement patterns, and position-specific risk factors.
ROLLOUT
The concept originated during an NFL visit to GE's innovation lab, where Jennifer Langton (SVP Player Health and Innovation) saw digital twin technology used to
predict turbine engine failures. The NFL evaluated Microsoft, Google, and AWS before selecting AWS as the technology partner. AWS had been the NFL's cloud
computing partner since 2017 through the Next Gen Stats platform. The Digital Athlete was developed through the NFL's $60M+ Engineering Roadmap, managed by
Football Research, Inc. (FRI). It was piloted with select teams in 2023, then deployed to all 32 NFL clubs for the 2024 season via a centralized team portal. The
portal provides each team with daily training volume data, individualized injury risk scores, and league-wide trends and benchmarks. Coaches and training staff use it
to manage practice intensity, in-season workloads, position-group fatigue, and individualized recovery protocols.
RESULT
In 2024, the NFL recorded a 17% reduction in concussions compared to 2023, the lowest concussion rate since the league began tracking. The platform also
informed the design of the new Dynamic Kickoff rule (reducing kickoff returns by 7%, projected to cut concussions from those plays by 15%) and drove
improvements in helmet design and manufacturing based on impact force data. The system identified position-specific injury patterns, such as quarterbacks being
more susceptible to rear head injuries when bracing the ball during a rush. Teams report using the platform for individualized training camp scheduling, weekly
practice intensity management, and soft-tissue injury prevention.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ACIS
This is the single closest structural comparable to ACIS. Both are league-wide AI-powered injury prevention platforms deployed to all teams via a centralized portal
with team-specific and league-wide benchmarking. The key difference: AWS likely subsidizes Digital Athlete costs in exchange for brand/marketing value as a
$100B+ company. ACIS should price at full standalone value since Baseline does not have that dynamic. The 17% concussion reduction is the strongest available
proof point that league-wide AI injury prevention produces measurable, documentable outcomes.

CLOSEST ACIS COMPARABLE
Second Spectrum + NBA ~$42M/yr est.
6-year deal (2016-2022) | AI tracking and analytics platform | All 30 NBA teams | Acquired by Genius Sports for $200M
WHAT IT IS
Second Spectrum was an AI analytics company founded in 2013 by three USC computer science professors. It developed the 'Eagle' machine learning platform for
real-time player and ball tracking, using camera-based optical tracking systems installed in all 30 NBA arenas. The system captured every player movement and ball
trajectory, then applied machine learning to generate advanced analytics: shot probability, defensive positioning effectiveness, play classification, and predictive
modeling. Second Spectrum served as the NBA's official optical tracking provider, replacing the previous SportVU system. It also expanded to the English Premier
League (EPL) and Major League Soccer (MLS) before being acquired by Genius Sports for $200M in 2021.
ROLLOUT
The NBA announced a combined $250M, 6-year partnership with both Sportradar (data distribution) and Second Spectrum (tracking + analytics) in 2016. Second
Spectrum's share was estimated at roughly $20-25M/year for the base league contract. Camera hardware was installed in all 30 NBA arenas. The critical innovation
was a tiered pricing architecture: a base tier of raw tracking data and standard analytics was included in the league contract for all 30 teams. A premium tier offering
advanced machine learning capabilities, custom modeling, and deeper predictive analytics was sold as a per-team add-on. 26 of 30 NBA teams (87%) paid extra for
the premium tier, demonstrating strong demand for AI-powered analytics beyond baseline monitoring. When the NBA restructured its tracking ecosystem in 2023, the
technology stack was unbundled: Sony's Hawk-Eye took over raw optical tracking, Sportradar continued data distribution (at a dramatically higher $1B+ price), and
Genius Sports (which had acquired Second Spectrum) retained the team-facing analytics layer.
RESULT
Second Spectrum became the foundational analytics infrastructure for the entire NBA. Its data powered team analytics departments, broadcast augmented reality
graphics, and league-wide research initiatives. The 87% premium upgrade rate validated that teams will pay substantially more for advanced AI capabilities beyond
basic monitoring. When the NBA restructured in 2023, the analytics layer retained standalone commercial value separate from raw tracking hardware, proving that
the intelligence layer (what ACIS represents) has independent pricing power.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ACIS
The tiered pricing architecture is the ideal template for ACIS: base monitoring for all 30 MLB teams under the league contract, premium predictive analytics as a
per-team upsell. The 87% upgrade rate (26/30 teams) proves teams will pay more for advanced AI beyond baseline. The $200M acquisition by Genius Sports
validates standalone value for AI analytics companies in sports.
