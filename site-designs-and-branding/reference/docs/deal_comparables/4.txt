TRACKING INFRASTRUCTURE
Hawk-Eye (Sony) + MLB Statcast Est. $20-80M/yr
Multi-year (2020-present) | Camera-based ball and player tracking | All 30 MLB + 60+ MiLB stadiums
WHAT IT IS
Hawk-Eye Innovations, owned by Sony Group Corporation, provides the tracking technology that powers MLB's Statcast system. Hawk-Eye replaced TrackMan as
MLB's official tracking provider starting in 2020. The system uses 12 high-resolution cameras per stadium to track ball flight (pitch velocity, spin rate, movement),
player positioning, sprint speed, and defensive metrics in real-time. In October 2024, Sony acquired KinaTrax, Inc., a markerless biomechanical motion capture
company that was already deployed in 100+ professional stadiums. This acquisition consolidated ball tracking and biomechanical analysis under one corporate
entity.
ROLLOUT
MLB centrally procured and deployed Hawk-Eye across all 30 MLB stadiums, 60+ minor league venues, and 25+ spring training facilities. The deployment was
managed by MLB's Commissioner's Office (led by CTO Jason Gaedtke), ensuring equal technology access for all teams. The transition from TrackMan to Hawk-Eye
was phased across venues. Hawk-Eye's EPL precedent provides a per-venue benchmark: approximately $250K+ per stadium installation, though MLB's system is
substantially more complex (12 cameras vs. goal-line only). The Sony/KinaTrax acquisition in 2024 added markerless biomechanical tracking to the existing camera
infrastructure.
RESULT
Hawk-Eye/Statcast powers the entire MLB analytics ecosystem used by every team, broadcast partner, and fan-facing product. It generates the underlying pitch
tracking, exit velocity, sprint speed, and defensive positioning data that has transformed how teams evaluate players and make in-game decisions. The data is the
raw material that systems like ACIS would consume and process.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ACIS
ACIS sits on top of Hawk-Eye/Statcast data rather than competing with it. This is complementary infrastructure. ACIS is the intelligence layer that makes Statcast
biomechanical data actionable for injury prevention, which neither Hawk-Eye nor KinaTrax currently does at scale. The Sony/KinaTrax consolidation is a competitive
landscape factor to monitor, but it reinforces that there is no existing league-wide injury prevention analytics layer.

TRACKING INFRASTRUCTURE
Zebra Technologies + NFL Est. low tens of millions/yr
12+ consecutive seasons (2014-present) | RFID/UWB player tracking | All 32 NFL teams | Rolling 3-year renewals
WHAT IT IS
Zebra Technologies Corporation (NASDAQ: ZBRA) provides RFID and ultra-wideband (UWB) tracking chips embedded in every NFL player's shoulder pads. The
system tracks real-time location, speed, acceleration, distance traveled, and acceleration for all players on every play. This data feeds into the NFL's Next Gen Stats
platform (powered by AWS) and is a foundational input to the Digital Athlete injury prediction system. Zebra upgraded from RFID to higher-precision UWB technology
during the partnership.
ROLLOUT
Zebra's tracking system has been deployed league-wide across all 32 NFL teams since 2014. The partnership has been renewed consistently in 3-year increments,
now running for 12+ consecutive seasons. The technology upgrade from RFID to UWB demonstrates how hardware partnerships evolve to deliver higher-fidelity
data over time. The per-venue deployment includes hardware installation, ongoing maintenance, and data processing/transmission.
RESULT
Zebra created the foundational player tracking data layer that powers every NFL analytics product, from broadcast graphics to the Digital Athlete injury prediction
platform. Once embedded in league operations and integrated into team workflows, the technology became deeply entrenched and virtually impossible to replace.
The 12+ year unbroken relationship demonstrates the stickiness of league-wide tracking infrastructure.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ACIS
Less directly relevant since ACIS is a software/analytics layer, not hardware. But the 12+ year sustained partnership demonstrates a critical principle: once tracking
infrastructure is embedded in league operations, it becomes indispensable. Get in early, become the standard, and the relationship compounds over time.
