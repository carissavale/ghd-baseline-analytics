MLB-Incubated Technology

BAMTech / MLB Advanced Media $3.83B+ total
2000-2022 | MLB-incubated tech company spun off and acquired by Disney | Now: Disney Streaming Services
WHAT IT IS
MLB Advanced Media (MLBAM) was created in 2000 by Commissioner Bud Selig to consolidate the digital media rights of all 30 MLB teams under one umbrella.
What started as a website and ticket-selling operation evolved into the most advanced live video streaming technology in the world, years ahead of Netflix, YouTube,
or any competitor. MLBAM developed proprietary streaming infrastructure that could deliver live video to millions of concurrent users with broadcast-level reliability.
By 2014, it had streamed over 2 billion live games and powered platforms including WatchESPN, HBO Now, the 2014 FIFA World Cup, NHL, PGA Tour, and WWE
Network. In 2015, the streaming technology division was spun off as BAMTech, which Disney ultimately acquired to power Disney+, Hulu, and ESPN+.
ROLLOUT
2000: Selig asks all 30 MLB teams to invest $1M/year for four years, creating a $120M funding target. Revenue came faster than expected, and only $77M of the
original $120M was ever needed. 2002: First-ever MLB game streamed online (Yankees vs. Rangers, August 26). Three years before YouTube was founded. 2003:
MLB.tv launched at $79.95/season, signing 100,000 subscribers. 2005: MLBAM acquires Tickets.com for $66M. 2008-2014: MLBAM expands to power streaming for
CBS March Madness, WatchESPN, HBO Now, WWE Network, PGA Tour, and others. Forbes calls it 'the biggest media company you've never heard of' in 2014.
February 2015: Streaming division spun off as BAMTech. August 2015: NHL signs 6-year, $600M deal with BAMTech, receiving a 10% equity stake. August 2016:
Disney acquires 33% stake for $1B. August 2017: Disney acquires additional 42% for $1.58B, bringing total to 75% controlling stake. Bob Iger calls it 'the beginning
of the reinvention of the Walt Disney Company.' BAMTech rebranded as Disney Streaming Services. August 2021: Disney buys NHL's 10% stake for $350M.
November 2022: Disney buys MLB's remaining 15% stake for $900M. Disney now owns 100%.
RESULT
BAMTech became the technology backbone of the entire Disney streaming empire, powering Disney+, Hulu, and ESPN+. MLB's initial $77M investment across 30
teams generated a return of approximately $2.55B+ in sale proceeds, roughly $85M per team and a 33x return on invested capital. Each MLB team received
approximately $33M from the initial Disney investment (2016) and an additional ~$52M from the subsequent transactions. The BAMTech story represents the single
most successful technology investment in the history of professional sports and fundamentally shaped MLB's approach to technology partnerships. It proved that
league-incubated technology, when properly scaled, can generate returns that dwarf the original investment by orders of magnitude.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ACIS
BAMTech is the foundational case study for how MLB thinks about technology: build or invest early, retain equity, and capture upside. MLB will expect a similar
dynamic in any ACIS partnership. They will want equity participation, not just a vendor-customer relationship. The BAMTech precedent also establishes that MLB is
willing to act as a centralized technology buyer on behalf of all 30 teams, deploying league-wide infrastructure rather than leaving it to individual clubs. This is exactly
the deployment model ACIS would use.
